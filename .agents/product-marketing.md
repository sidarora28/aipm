## The product

**AI PM Accelerator — Cohort 4**

8-week live cohort program for product managers who want to design, build, and ship
a real AI product. Not self-paced. Not video-only. One live session per week
(90–120 min), a deliverable every week, and a working product at a real URL by Demo Day.

**Cohort 4 starts: 15 October 2026.**

Instructor: Sid Arora — Head of Product for Gen AI at Yelp, founder of JustAnotherPM.

### Curriculum spine

| Week | Theme | Core skill |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The AI PM Role | 7 Questions Framework |
| 2 | Basics of AI | Context Engineering |
| 3 | Do We Need AI? | 6 AI Patterns + Red Flags |
| 4 | AI Product Sense | AI Product Teardown |
| 5 | Design & Evals | AI-First PRD + Eval Framework |
| 6 | Build with Lovable / Claude Code | Ship a working AI thin slice |
| 7 | Testing & Launch Readiness | Testing Loop + 4 Audits |
| 8 | Demo Day | Live product demo |

### The four proprietary frameworks

These are the IP. Lead with them — they are the thing no competitor can copy.

1. **7 Questions Framework** (Week 1) — the operating model for any AI product decision
2. **5-Gate AI Decision Framework / 6 AI Patterns** (Week 3) — when to say no to AI
3. **AI-First PRD Template** (Week 5) — 9 sections built for probabilistic products
4. **Eval Framework** (Week 5) — dimensions, golden set, LLM-as-judge, 1–4 scoring

> The Eval Framework is the single strongest differentiator. It is the skill most AI
> courses omit entirely, and it is the one hiring managers actually probe for.

### What they leave with

- A live, deployed AI product at a real URL
- A completed AI-First PRD and Eval Framework for that product
- A 2–3 minute demo video usable in job applications
- Lifetime access to recordings, frameworks, templates, pre-reads
- Permanent access to the alumni Slack across every cohort

## ICP

**Primary:** Product managers with 3+ years experience who:
- Feel behind on AI and don't know where to start
- Use ChatGPT daily but have never built an AI product
- Work somewhere with AI on the roadmap and want to lead it, not watch it
- Need credible proof of work for an AI PM interview

**Secondary:** PMs already working on AI who picked it up haphazardly and want the
frameworks to fill in blind spots — especially evals.

**Explicitly not for:** ML engineers, PMs brand new to product, anyone wanting
self-paced with no live interaction, anyone unwilling to put in 4–6 hrs/week.

### Geography

Cohort 1–2 ran two live session tracks: North America (~11am–12pm EST) and
Europe/India (~12pm GMT / 4:30pm IST). Meta acquisition data shows meaningful
India, USA, and UK segments — ad sets are already split that way.

## Pain points, ranked

Ranked by how often they appear in Cohort 1 verbatims. Lead ads and emails with 1–3.

1. **Scattered knowledge, no framework.** They've consumed hours of AI content and
   still can't answer a basic AI product question in a meeting. The pain is not
   ignorance — it's incoherence.
2. **Never built anything.** Deep identity pain, especially for 10+ year PMs. "I have
   not written a single piece of code" carries shame, not neutrality.
3. **Falling behind, visibly.** AI is on their company roadmap and someone else is
   leading it.
4. **Nothing to show in interviews.** Theoretical AI knowledge is now table stakes; a
   shipped product is not.
5. **Can't tell good AI opportunities from bad ones.** They say yes to AI initiatives
   that should have been rules-based, and can't argue the case with evidence.
6. **Overwhelm and pace anxiety.** "Without losing my mental sanity."

## Objections, ranked

Handle in this order across the cart-open sequence.

1. **"$1,495 is a lot."** → Answer with the Milo quote (higher-visibility projects
   directly after) and the portfolio piece. Never discount to answer this — the PPP
   tier already exists for genuine affordability, and it is regional, not negotiable.
2. **"I can't code."** → This is the *qualification*, not the disqualification. Karthik
   and Tushar quotes.
3. **"I don't have 4–6 hours a week."** → Be honest. It is 4–6 hours, 8–10 in build
   weeks. The students who treat deliverables as real work get the outcome. Do not
   soften this — under-selling effort produces refunds.
4. **"I can learn this free on YouTube."** → Natalie's quote answers it exactly.
   Free content gives bits and pieces. The course gives the framework, the deadline,
   and the room.
5. **"Timezone won't work."** → Two live tracks, all sessions recorded within 24h.
6. **"Is this just another AI course?"** → Seven live student URLs. Show, don't argue.

## Voice

Sid's copy runs through the stack: `internet-adcopy` → `emotion-evoker` →
`next-episode` → `humanizer`. House style is Nicolas Cole / Ship 30 for 30.

**Rules:**
- Give away the information for free. Charge for access, mentorship, organisation,
  implementation, and accountability.
- Concrete before abstract. "My French is better than my SQL" beats "non-technical PMs".
- Never name the emotion. Show the evidence that produces it.
- No hype, no fake urgency, no invented statistics. The real numbers are strong enough.
- Lead with the problem, not the product — the same rule taught in Week 8.

**Banned:** "unlock", "supercharge", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world",
"revolutionary", any countdown that isn't real, any testimonial that isn't verbatim.
